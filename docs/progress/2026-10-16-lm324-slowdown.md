# 2026-10-16: Faster dense LU (two LM324s made the app chug)

Branch `claude/lm324-slowdown-26w4mv`. Gady built two small op-amp demos (ideal and LM324, +6 V
and +5 V in, a resistor to ground, LM324 on ±12 V), copied them, and with a second LM324 the
whole app slowed down.

## Cause

The LM324 ("fixed" model, `OpAmpRealElm` model 2) is a 69-row nonlinear matrix. Every step
factors it with upstream's dense Crout LU: about 107,000 multiply-adds, of which only about 3,700
have two nonzero factors. One LM324 cost about 250 µs per step and two about 510 µs; at the
circuit's speed (1632 steps per second) two need 83% of a core, so frames hit the 50 ms budget and
the app drew at 11 fps. Upstream runs the same dense loops (ENGINE-NOTES section 6), so it should
behave the same (inferred from the source; the reference build couldn't fetch GWT from Maven in
this container).

## Done

- `luFactorDense` skips the products with a zero factor (column lists of L, same order of
  subtractions), and falls back to upstream's loops if anything turns infinite or NaN. Results are
  bit-identical: unit tests against `luFactorDenseFull`, goldens pass, and node voltages of all 373
  examples over 1000 steps hash the same before and after. No deviation entry.
- Matrix rows are created as doubles from the start, and the per-iteration matrix copy reads each
  row once.
- Measured (node, best of 15): one LM324 250 → 85 µs per step, two 510 → 172 µs. In the app
  (headless Chromium, Gady's circuit twice): 11 fps → 60 fps at full speed.

## Next (performance follow-ups, not done)

- The per-iteration copy of `origMatrix` into `matrix` and the backup copy in `luFactorDense` are
  now about a quarter of an LM324 step; `Float64Array` rows would make both a block copy.
- `luSolveDense` still walks all n² entries; it could reuse the sparse structure.
- `Expr.eval` (interpreted expressions of the LM324v2's VCVS/VCCS parts) and `TransistorElm.doStep`
  are the next element costs.
- `setNodeVoltages` recalculates each element's current once per connected post.
- Rendering wasn't profiled here.
