# 2026-10-16: Tidy up straightens wires and lines parts up

Branch `claude/project-thread-2yctz2`. Gady picked "Tidy up" from a list of new feature ideas:
one button that straightens wires, aligns parts and removes kinks.

## Done

- `packages/app/src/editor/tidy.ts`: the tidy pass. Plain wires are grouped into chains (runs
  through bare joints, ending at a part's post, a junction or a loose end). Each chain is
  redrawn as the simplest clear route: one straight wire, or an L. Exact duplicate chains and
  wire loops that join nothing go, as do zero-length wires. Then parts whose posts only meet
  such chains move up to two grid steps (any direction) when that makes their wires simpler,
  one best move at a time. A route never puts a bend or end on anything, never runs through a
  part's body, along another wire, or over a post; a wire that leaves a part sideways, or along
  its body, counts as an extra bend (not at junctions).
- What connects to what never changes: `Editor.tidyUp` compares a connectivity signature (net of
  every post, plus posts sitting on a wire's middle) before and after, and puts the circuit back
  if it differs. Wires a scope plots, routed wires and wires showing current or voltage
  differently from their chain stay as they are. Only plain wires are replaced and parts moved
  by whole grid steps, so the file format and CircuitJS1 compatibility are unchanged; no
  deviation entry.
- Acts on the selection when there is one, else the whole circuit. One undo step ("Tidy up").
- Edit menu and the empty-canvas right-click menu, with a toast: "Tidied up. Wires: 21 → 12.
  Parts moved: 4." or "Already tidy." Strings in all 16 languages (by Claude, not
  native-checked).
- Tests: unit cases (straight runs, staircases, slanted wires, junctions, routing around a part,
  duplicates, nudging a part, selection only, scope wires), and a sweep over every bundled
  example (upstream's 373 plus ours) checking connections are unchanged, no new wire crosses a
  part, and a second tidy finds nothing to do. 57 examples change; e2e `tidy.spec.ts`.
- Screenshots in the project folder `tidy-up/` (a messy op-amp buffer, sram, relay counter,
  shift register, before and after).

## Open issues

- Routes are straight or one L; a chain that needs two bends to get around something keeps
  its current shape.
- Parts sharing a post directly with another part (no wire between) don't move.
- In dense circuits (relay counter) the result has fewer wires but isn't always easier to read.
